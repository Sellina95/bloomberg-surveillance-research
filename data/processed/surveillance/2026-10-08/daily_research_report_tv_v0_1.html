EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
  • Global bond yields are facing upward pressure driven by robust economic activity, fiscal deficits, and structural shifts where investment outpaces savings.
  • Artificial intelligence infrastructure demand remains robust and durable over the next couple of years, though credit markets are beginning to show selective cracks and high yield dispersion.
  • Labor markets exhibit underlying strength and stability, supported by resilient capital investment and manufacturing activity despite higher borrowing costs.

RATES / BONDS

The global fixed income market is navigating elevated yields, supply pressures, and term premium concerns, though shorter-term maturities continue to offer attractive relative value.

KEY VIEWS

  • The belly of the U.S. Treasury curve is an optimal positioning area to capture income while avoiding the full impact of fiscal inflation and long-end term premium risks.
  • The front end of the yield curve offers historically attractive real rates and high income with low volatility.
  • Global rate increases are partly amplified by the unwinding of positions by speculators borrowing at near-zero rates in Japan.

RISKS

  • Sovereign debt sustainability concerns in Europe, with French bond yields approaching critical thresholds reminiscent of past debt crises.
  • Persistent large fiscal deficits driving up term premiums and pushing benchmark yields higher.
USD / FX

Foreign exchange dynamics are increasingly influenced by divergent global monetary policy paths, sovereign debt pressures, and the unwinding of historical carry trades.

KEY VIEWS

  • Unwinding of carry trades funded in Japan continues to impact global rate and currency dynamics as interest rates adjust.

RISKS

  • Fiscal and political instability in major economies exerting downward pressure on regional sovereign valuations.
EQUITIES

Equity markets face a tug-of-war between robust corporate cash flows, moderating inflation indicators, and the valuation pressures of rising benchmark bond yields.

KEY VIEWS

  • Falling inflation measures could remove key pressures on the bond market and help broaden market strength beyond mega-cap segments.
  • Strong corporate cash flow pipelines reduce the probability of severe default cycles and support overall equity market stability.

RISKS

  • Multiple contraction and increased volatility if 10-year Treasury yields rise significantly above nominal GDP growth rates.
CREDIT

Corporate credit markets are experiencing performance dispersion and widening spreads in select sectors, even as high-grade corporate borrowers exhibit solid fundamental health.

KEY VIEWS

  • High-grade corporate debt in certain regions is trading inside sovereign ratings due to strong balance sheet fundamentals.
  • Performance dispersion is visible in high-yield credit, with significant widening observed in lower-rated tech and media segments.
  • Hyperscalers are increasingly utilizing special purpose vehicles and private credit to manage capital requirements and concentration limits.

RISKS

  • Potential overbuilding and uncertain return-on-investment timelines for AI data center infrastructure leading to credit saturation.
AI / TECHNOLOGY

Semiconductor and AI infrastructure demand remains durable and capacity-constrained, though investors are scrutinizing the multi-year duration of the spending cycle.

KEY VIEWS

  • AI chip demand continues to outpace supply, with industry expansion bottlenecked by physical constraints like memory, foundry capacity, land, and power.
  • Major tech hyperscalers are borrowing to fund productive capital expenditures rather than stock buybacks.

RISKS

  • Growing investor concern over the long-term duration and financing sustainability of debt-funded AI infrastructure.
COMMODITIES

Energy markets remain vulnerable to geopolitical friction, transport disruptions, and supply constraints in key shipping corridors.

KEY VIEWS

  • Ongoing attacks on tankers and refining disruptions in the Middle East are driving up product and crude costs.
  • The executive branch has limited domestic policy levers to significantly reduce consumer fuel costs in the short term.

RISKS

  • Persistent Middle East conflict exacerbating energy price volatility and feeding broader inflationary pressures.

RESEARCH TAKEAWAYS

  • Labor market indicators remain resilient, reducing near-term employment deterioration risks but maintaining baseline inflationary stickiness.
  • Geopolitical and fiscal uncertainties warrant heightened vigilance regarding term premiums and energy corridor security.
