EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
  • Bond markets are offering generational intermediate yields near historic highs, presenting significant value opportunities despite subdued investor demand.
  • A major market dislocation persists between cautious consumer sentiment regarding high costs and corporate reports of record profits powered by artificial intelligence and data center infrastructure build-outs.
  • Regulatory and tax headwinds surrounding AI and data centers represent a key policy risk that could impact high-growth technology investments.
MACRO THEMES

Macroeconomic Dislocation and Growth Headwinds

SOURCE-DERIVED SYNTHESIS · PARAPHRASED

A distinct gap exists between consumer pressure from elevated prices and corporate enthusiasm over AI-driven revenue. Looking ahead to 2027, tough base effects, higher energy costs, and a tighter cost of capital threaten to slow economic momentum.

Matt Miskin - Manulife JH Investments

AI Infrastructure and Regulatory Risks

SOURCE-DERIVED SYNTHESIS · PARAPHRASED

The rapid scale-up of data centers and artificial intelligence faces evolving policy scrutiny, with debates centered on whether to rely on flexible industry self-regulation or impose stricter government mandates and targeted taxes.

Jennifer Huddleston - Cato Institute Matt Miskin - Manulife JH Investments

Intermediate fixed-income yields have reached attractive levels not observed in two decades, creating compelling valuation entry points despite low overall investor appetite.

KEY VIEWS

  • Intermediate bond yields are currently offering approximately 6%, representing a generational high-value opportunity.
  • Anticipated economic slowing and softer growth into subsequent years are expected to place downward pressure on bond yields.

RISKS

  • Continued investor apathy toward fixed-income products despite elevated yields.
  • Persistent energy and input costs keeping yields elevated in the near term before deceleration takes hold.

EQUITIES

Equity markets display a sharp divergence between buoyant corporate earnings driven by once-in-a-generation AI capital expenditure and consumer distress driven by high living and energy costs.

KEY VIEWS

  • Corporate management teams report stellar performance anchored by data center and AI build-outs, contrasting sharply with negative consumer sentiment regarding price levels.
  • High valuation bars and tough base effects pose looming earnings hurdles for 2027.

RISKS

  • Stiffer cost of capital and energy inputs acting as a drag on future corporate margins.
  • Potential legislative tax proposals targeting data centers and AI companies creating sudden valuation pressure.
CREDIT

Broader credit conditions are influenced by the corporate cost of capital, with high-yield opportunities mirroring the attractive valuations seen across intermediate fixed-income markets.

KEY VIEWS

  • Higher costs of capital across the economy will test corporate balance sheets as growth cools.

RISKS

  • Vulnerability to tightening financial conditions if growth softens faster than anticipated.
AI / TECHNOLOGY

The AI sector is experiencing historic infrastructure expansion supported by corporate capital spending, balanced against rising policy discussions over taxation and regulatory frameworks.

KEY VIEWS

  • Data center and AI infrastructure build-outs represent a once-in-a-generation economic driver supporting strong corporate profits.
  • Industry self-regulation and flexible best practices are viewed as essential for allowing fast-moving technology to evolve without premature stifling.
  • Existing legal frameworks already provide baseline remedies for targeted harms like fraud and discrimination.

RISKS

  • Proposed tax policies targeting data centers and AI operations could impose heavy structural burdens during a crucial development phase.
  • Washington policy anxiety could spur heavy-handed mandates or legislative overreach.
COMMODITIES

Energy and input costs remain a significant factor influencing both consumer sentiment and corporate cost structures.

KEY VIEWS

  • Elevated energy input costs continue to impact consumer purchasing power and corporate expense baselines.

RISKS

  • Continued energy price pressures compounding broader macroeconomic deceleration risks.
