RATES / BONDS

Central bank policy remains restrictive as policymakers weigh persistent core inflation above target against softening sectors like housing.

KEY VIEWS

  • Starting points matter for inflation, keeping additional interest rate hikes under consideration rather than immediate rate cuts.
  • Global real money investors face attractive alternative yields, increasing the risk of capital rotation away from vulnerable sovereign debt markets lacking fiscal anchors.

RISKS

  • Hawkish central bank surprises leading to tighter-than-expected financial conditions.
  • Sovereign debt pressures and widening bond spreads in European markets lacking political fiscal cohesion.
USD / FX

Foreign exchange markets are increasingly sensitive to divergent global real yields and fiscal stability risks across major economic blocs.

KEY VIEWS

  • Higher global real yields provide strong alternatives for international capital, putting pressure on currencies tied to deteriorating fiscal profiles.
  • Political friction within the eurozone complicates potential sovereign support mechanisms.

RISKS

  • Capital outflows driven by sovereign debt concerns and political polarization.
EQUITIES

Equity markets are experiencing a rotation away from concentrated tech leadership toward defensive sectors, cash-generative value stocks, and companies with robust free cash flow.

KEY VIEWS

  • Profit margins face downward pressure as inflation secondary effects and higher operating costs work their way through corporate earnings.
  • Corporations demonstrating disciplined capital allocation and strong buyback programs are finding favor amid broader market volatility.

RISKS

  • Earnings disappointments in high-valuation growth sectors spilling over into the broader equity market.
  • Regulatory and policy interventions impacting corporate profitability.
CREDIT

Credit markets are showing increased discernment as tightening financing costs separate durable market leaders from marginal borrowers.

KEY VIEWS

  • Credit spreads are starting to reflect capital constraints, heightening the differentiation between strong balance sheets and vulnerable operators.

RISKS

  • Spike in refinancing costs for leveraged entities unable to pass rising expenses onto consumers.
AI / TECHNOLOGY

The artificial intelligence theme is maturing as higher financing costs push companies toward quality leaders, open-weight models, and efficient compute strategies.

KEY VIEWS

  • Rising debt expenses are increasing the likelihood of equity issuance among cash-burning technology players.
  • Financial institutions are optimizing AI operational demand through third-party tokens and open-weight models while fiercely guarding against cybersecurity and consumer liability risks.

RISKS

  • State-level regulatory moratoriums and anti-data center sentiment posing severe operational hurdles.
  • Uncertainty surrounding long-term business model monetization and cash flow visibility for heavy AI investments.
COMMODITIES

Traditional energy and midstream assets are regaining investor appeal supported by consistent hydrocarbon demand and infrastructure requirements.

KEY VIEWS

  • Midstream pipelines and service sectors offering consistent cash flows are becoming more investable as traditional energy demand holds firm.

RISKS

  • Short-term traction volatility in energy service markets associated with geopolitical rebuild cycles.
