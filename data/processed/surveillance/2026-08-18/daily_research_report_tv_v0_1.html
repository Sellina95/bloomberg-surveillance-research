RATES / BONDS

While Treasury interventions such as long-end buybacks provide temporary dealer liquidity, structural fiscal deficits, above-expectation inflation, and resilient economic growth continue to drive elevated real yields.

KEY VIEWS

  • Attempting to manipulate or control the 30-year yield is a short-term measure that the Treasury cannot sustain without the Federal Reserve (Patrick Armstrong).
  • Treasury buyback operations improve market liquidity and clear risk from dealer balance sheets better than cutting auction sizes (Ira Jersey).
  • Current 10-year Treasury yields offer an attractive opportunity for investors to lock in 5% returns (Emily Roland).

RISKS

  • Heavy reliance on short-term T-bills creates severe refinancing risk if the Federal Reserve is forced to hike rates (Ira Jersey).
  • The backup in long-end yields driven by term premium and external supply factors like AI issuance could trigger a broader equity selloff (Molly Brooks).
USD / FX

The U.S. dollar has dropped to a three-month low, acting as a crucial liquidity release valve for risk assets. Meanwhile, the euro faces downward pressure despite strong European equity momentum and selective carry-trade performance.

KEY VIEWS

  • The U.S. dollar hitting a three-month low is fueling debasement fear trades into gold and alternative assets (Dirk Willer).
  • The U.S. maintains intrinsic advantages as the world's foremost reserve currency and asset (Geoffrey Yu).
  • Carry trades can outperform and European equity momentum can continue, but this does not translate to a stronger euro against the dollar or across crosses (Geoffrey Yu).

RISKS

  • A sudden reversal in dollar weakness could remove the liquidity buffer supporting global risk assets (Emily Roland).
EQUITIES

Equity markets continue to ride strong momentum and impressive earnings, but high equity valuations must work harder to compete against attractive fixed-income yields and rising capital expenditure pressures.

KEY VIEWS

  • Equity investors remain focused on strong earnings and momentum, though markets tend to go up steadily and drop sharply (Jack Caffrey).
  • Attractive fixed-income yields force equity investors to work harder to justify competing stock valuations (Julie Biel).
  • Nvidia's fundamental constraint is growing competition from alternative custom silicon solutions, despite near-term doubling of revenue and earnings (Gil Luria).

RISKS

  • Rising long-term bond yields and compressed valuation multiples could trigger a sharp momentum pullback in equity markets (Patrick Armstrong, Jack Caffrey).
CREDIT

Corporate debt issuance is surging to fund AI infrastructure and supply chain reshoring, testing corporate cash flows while fixed-income alternatives offer compelling yields.

KEY VIEWS

  • There is a huge amount of debt issuance from Corporate America to fund capital expenditures and reshoring (Patrick Armstrong, Jack Caffrey).
  • Some fixed-income credits are viewed by investors as safer than U.S. equities, offering juicy yields that heighten competition for capital (Julie Biel).

RISKS

  • Elevated corporate debt loads coupled with high financing costs could test balance sheets if AI revenue payoffs are delayed.
AI / TECHNOLOGY

Artificial intelligence capital expenditures are driven by existential corporate game theory, though questions regarding long-term returns, circular financing, and rising competition persist.

KEY VIEWS

  • Heavy AI spending is treated as an existential necessity by corporations, unconstrained by current financing costs (Jack Caffrey).
  • Nvidia's direct customer ecosystem funding via circular financing represents a small fraction of its overall revenue and profits (Gil Luria).
  • Long-term questions remain regarding whether AI will generate high returns or mirror low-return utility infrastructure booms like airlines (Julie Biel).

RISKS

  • Potential multiple compression if AI infrastructure investments fail to yield adequate revenue returns by 2027 (Julie Biel, Gil Luria).
