RATES / BONDS

Long-end yields face upward pressure from heavy sovereign and corporate bond supply, structural inflation, and shifting real interest rate expectations, though some analysts argue supply angst is largely priced into spreads.

KEY VIEWS

  • A coordinated operation between the Federal Reserve and the Treasury targeting the long end of the curve could successfully absorb high issuance volumes without harming the broader economy.
  • The recent selloff in long-end rates reflects higher real yields driven by productivity and growth expectations rather than a complete loss of central bank credibility.
  • Reaching a 10-year Treasury yield of 5% would represent a compelling multi-year income-locking opportunity for investors.

RISKS

  • Failure of leadership to clarify reaction functions could push long-end yields testing 5.50% or higher.
  • Persistent inflation and aggressive policy inaction could cause market participants to push back against the central bank and drive inflation expectations higher.
USD / FX

Foreign trade negotiations and currency strategies remain influenced by domestic political timelines, potential policy shifts, and protectionist industrial lobbying.

KEY VIEWS

  • International trade partners and foreign entities may stall negotiations with the U.S. in anticipation of upcoming midterm elections.
  • Domestic industrial pressure from steel, auto, and aluminum sectors strongly supports maintaining protective tariff structures.

RISKS

  • Prolonged trade policy uncertainty could introduce cross-border economic friction and sector-specific volatility.
EQUITIES

Technology and semiconductor sectors experience strong fundamental revenue growth paired with occasional volatility and pullbacks in supply chain components.

KEY VIEWS

  • Recent pullbacks of up to 50% in select AI supply chain and memory names present attractive long-term entry points for secular growth exposure.
  • Customer diversification is expanding beyond top hyperscalers toward non-hyperscalers, broadening the revenue base for hardware providers.

RISKS

  • Elevated capital expenditure without immediate productivity payoffs could eventually lead investors to question financial returns and create market skittishness.
CREDIT

Investors find better risk-adjusted cushions in credit and mortgage sectors compared to pure sovereigns due to attractive yield spreads.

KEY VIEWS

  • Corporate credit spreads and rates have largely priced in near-term supply concerns and macroeconomic friction.
  • Yield spreads in credit and mortgage products offer a better buffer against duration risk than sovereign bonds alone.

RISKS

  • Spillover volatility from persistent sovereign supply pressures and rate uncertainty could impact broader spread product stability.
AI / TECHNOLOGY

Artificial intelligence infrastructure demand remains robust, driven by surging needs for inference capacity and custom silicon adoption.

KEY VIEWS

  • Google's TPU sales represent a highly significant and underappreciated driver of cloud infrastructure monetization in a capacity-constrained environment.
  • Nvidia's revenue is projected to experience massive multi-year expansion, supported by severe shortages in inference capacity and strong non-hyperscaler demand growth.
  • High-profile leadership exits at major tech firms are viewed as manageable over the long term given deep talent benches.

RISKS

  • Capacity constraints and high capital expenditure intensity could strain supply chains or face scrutiny if end-market productivity realization lags.
COMMODITIES

Global energy security requires multi-year domestic infrastructure investments, while economic sanctions face legal and operational delays.

KEY VIEWS

  • Regions like Canada and the U.S. must prioritize domestic energy infrastructure, including local refining and pipelines, under a multi-year security plan.
  • Enforcing complex economic sanctions requires extensive legal documentation and time, limiting their immediate impact on global oil flows.

RISKS

  • Physical commodity shortages and complex sanction enforcement timelines can keep energy prices elevated and complicate broader disinflation trends.
