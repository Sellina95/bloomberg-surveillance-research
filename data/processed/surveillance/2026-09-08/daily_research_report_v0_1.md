## Rates / Bonds

### Summary

Fixed income markets face upward pressure on yields driven by robust nominal economic growth, heavy corporate and hyperscaler debt issuance, and potential mortgage convexity hedging risks.

### Key Views

- Long-term bond yields are increasingly reacting to fiscal spending and high-quality corporate issuance rather than near-term central bank actions alone.
- Global yield curve steepness positions U.S. 30-year bonds for potential severe selloffs, exacerbated by constrained dealer liquidity during mortgage convexity hedging.
- Persistent inflation prints above consensus provide the Federal Reserve with justification to execute multiple rate hikes.

### Risks

- Violent selloffs in long-duration debt due to mortgage convexity hedging and insufficient dealer liquidity.
- Uncontrolled surges in Treasury yields approaching technical breaking points if rate-hiking campaigns expand.


## USD / FX

### Summary

Foreign exchange markets navigate diverging central bank tightening cycles, with the Bank of Japan facing pressure to lift rates despite global economic headwinds.

### Key Views

- The Bank of Japan has room to raise interest rates to protect the currency and stabilize debt positions, though global rate-hiking cycles complicate aggressive carry-trade unwinds.
- Persistent U.S. economic resilience and sticky inflation leave little justification for immediate monetary easing.

### Risks

- Intervention strategies and shifting carry-trade dynamics inducing sharp volatility in dollar-yen exchange rates.
- Sustained global energy price pressures weakening foreign currencies relative to the U.S. dollar.


## Equities

### Summary

Equity markets remain supported by positive 12-month forward earnings growth and manageable valuations, though tightening financial conditions and political noise pose headwinds.

### Key Views

- Equities remain resilient as long as forward earnings growth stays positive and investor sentiment avoids extreme euphoria.
- Transition toward tighter financial conditions and potential policy errors threaten multiple expansion and create a less conducive environment for broad equity gains.

### Risks

- Aggressive multi-rate hike campaigns by the Federal Reserve triggering equity market corrections.
- Supply-side energy shocks and rising input costs squeezing consumer discretionary spending and corporate margins.


## Credit

### Summary

Corporate credit conditions reflect growing strain on higher-leveraged balance sheets amidst elevated borrowing costs and heavy issuance volume.

### Key Views

- Higher interest rates are increasingly impacting corporate balance sheets, particularly among higher-levered borrowers.
- Hyperscalers maintain large debt issuance programs, creating high-quality supply that influences long-term yield structures.

### Risks

- Deterioration in lower-rated credit tiers as higher borrowing costs persist.
- Bond market rejections of financing for over-leveraged participants in the technology and AI sectors.


## AI / Technology

### Summary

The artificial intelligence buildout faces physical and regulatory friction, which serves to regulate the investment cycle and prevent speculative excess.

### Key Views

- Local community pushback, grid connection delays, and power equipment shortages are acting as natural regulators on data center construction.
- Autonomous AI agents present critical security vulnerabilities, heightening calls for robust regulatory oversight to protect financial and payment systems.
- The eventual termination point of the equity bull cycle will likely be tied to either a hyperscaler capital expenditure pullback or bond market pushback against AI financing.

### Risks

- Severe security breaches and hacking incidents driven by autonomous AI agents.
- Prolonged municipal disputes and power supply bottlenecks causing sharp decelerations in technology infrastructure deployment.


## Commodities

### Summary

Refined products and diesel markets experience extreme tightness due to refining capacity damage, low inventories, and high operational input costs.

### Key Views

- Diesel represents the tightest energy market, exacerbated by historical export losses from Russia and refinery yield shifts away from gasoline.
- Elevated natural gas costs in Europe and unresolved capacity constraints in the Middle East keep refining costs high and product margins elevated.
- U.S. strategic efforts to bypass the Strait of Hormuz face hurdles regarding the export volume of refined products, fertilizer, and aluminum.

### Risks

- Escalating geopolitical tensions in the Persian Gulf threatening global energy supplies.
- Persistent high crack spreads driving sustained inflationary pressures for industrial and consumer transport sectors.

- **Trigger:** Hotter-than-expected CPI inflation prints and persistent wage-price pressures.
- **Market Impact:** Sharp equity market corrections, severe upward pressure on bond yields, and tighter financial conditions across consumer and corporate sectors.

### Refined Energy Supply Bottlenecks

- **Trigger:** Persistent capacity damage in refining hubs and escalating geopolitical friction in the Persian Gulf.
- **Market Impact:** Sustained high diesel and crack spread prices, driving input cost inflation and margin compression for industrial users.

### AI Infrastructure Spending Deceleration

- **Trigger:** Local community pushback, grid power limitations, or bond market rejection of tech sector financing.
- **Market Impact:** Downward pressure on long-term Treasury yields through economic slowdown impulses, alongside sector-specific valuation adjustments in technology equities.
