RATES / BONDS

Treasury debt management strategies face structural friction, with recommendations to phase out the 20-year bond and reallocate issuance to front-end liquidity pools, while fiscal deficit trajectories continue upward.

KEY VIEWS

  • The US Treasury should eliminate the 20-year bond and reallocate issuance to the front end to leverage heavy money market liquidity.
  • Structural fiscal deficits and debt-to-GDP ratios are projected to climb steadily without an immediate forcing mechanism from the bond market.
  • The Federal Reserve's meeting calendar is misaligned with key macroeconomic data releases (CPI, GDP, PCE), and moving to a six-meeting schedule with improved transparency could reduce policy communication hurdles.

RISKS

  • A political sweep scenario could exacerbate borrowing and spending concerns, increasing supply pressure on long-end duration.
  • Misaligned meeting schedules and blackout windows can create avoidable market volatility and communication challenges around major economic data drops.

EQUITIES

Equities generally remain favored over credit for growth-oriented portfolios despite high single-digit yields in quality bond portfolios, though tech sectors face earnings momentum slowdowns and late-cycle capex pressures.

KEY VIEWS

  • Equities remain a more attractive investment than credit despite high single-digit yields available in quality bond portfolios.
  • The tech sector is experiencing a transition away from paying up for hard capex toward hyperscalers benefiting from broader software agents and compute usage.
  • State and local legislation, such as regional tax policies and data center regulations, are increasingly important for portfolio positioning.

RISKS

  • Earnings momentum in technology has slowed down, and heavy initial financing needs through 2027 could weigh on equity valuations.
  • State-level regulatory changes and governor races introduce localized policy risks for infrastructure and tech investments.
CREDIT

Credit spreads and corporate issuance remain resilient with disciplined issuer behavior, though bond investors maintain a defensive stance on AI-related debt due to a lack of equity upside participation.

KEY VIEWS

  • Corporate issuers are demonstrating increased sensitivity and discipline regarding deal sizing and timing, helping insulate spreads.
  • Hyperscaler investment-grade debt issuance has expanded to roughly $450-$500 billion annually, matching the scale of U.S. Treasury 20-year and 30-year issuance.
  • Bond investors remain cautious on AI sector debt because fixed income holders do not participate in the underlying equity economics.

RISKS

  • The shift of hyperscalers from cash flow positive to free cash flow negative raises credit concerns regarding future debt servicing and capex discipline.
  • Persistent heavy debt supply from tech infrastructure build-out could continue putting upward pressure on corporate yields.
AI / TECHNOLOGY

The artificial intelligence trade is shifting from a hardware-heavy capex boom toward an emphasis on software agents, compute demand, and hyperscaler positioning, though valuation corrections and financing needs create near-term friction.

KEY VIEWS

  • Hyperscalers are transitioning from cash flow positive to free cash flow negative as they absorb massive capital expenditure requirements.
  • While some analysts view semiconductors as facing a late-cycle capex squeeze and potential 'dead money' status through 2027, others see recent pullbacks as an attractive secular valuation discount.
  • Declining token prices reflect engineering improvements and open-source evolution rather than fundamental economic failure.

RISKS

  • Lack of capital discipline among hyperscalers risks creating oversupply and margin compression across hardware supply chains.
  • Estimated cumulative financing needs for AI infrastructure through 2027 could strain capital markets and pressure tech valuations.
COMMODITIES

Crude oil is supported by tight inventories and geopolitical conflicts, while European energy markets face significant winter security risks.

KEY VIEWS

  • Crude oil prices remaining around $100 per barrel through 2027 represent a reasonable target given ongoing geopolitical disruptions and tight inventories.
  • European energy markets face severe risks heading into winter due to potential supply cutoffs and volatile weather conditions.

RISKS

  • Volatile winter weather could disrupt European energy supplies and drive sharp price spikes.
  • Geopolitical flare-ups in oil-producing regions could maintain a high structural risk premium in commodity markets.
