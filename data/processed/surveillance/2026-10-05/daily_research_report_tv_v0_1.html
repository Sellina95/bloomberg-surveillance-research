EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
  • Global financial markets are navigating a structural shift toward positive real interest rates and a post-sub-2% inflation era, with corporate earnings growth remaining robust despite tighter monetary conditions.
  • Extreme equity market concentration, reminiscent of the late 1990s, continues to polarize market participants between concentrated mega-cap/semiconductor leadership and widespread underperformance in small caps.
  • Geopolitical shipping risks in the Middle East and sovereign fiscal pressures—particularly in France—keep energy input costs and regional debt dynamics elevated, overshadowing superficial supply-side policy interventions.

RATES / BONDS

Fixed-income markets face ongoing structural pressures from fiscal deficits and heavy AI-related capital expenditure, though attractive math on 10-year durations and potential long-term yield peaks introduce complex positioning dynamics.

KEY VIEWS

  • Long-term Treasury yields, including the 30-year yield, are viewed by some as on a trajectory toward 6% due to structural fiscal imbalances, while others anticipate yields moving lower over a 12-to-18-month horizon.
  • Buying 10-year Treasuries offers favorable math where yield-to-maturity exceeds duration, providing a cushion against moderate further yield increases.
  • Monetary policy transmission lags mean tighter conditions are continuing to weigh on interest-rate-sensitive sectors like housing.

RISKS

  • Rapidly accelerating long-term yields increasing debt expense pressures and risking a macro data trap.
  • Uncertainty regarding future Federal Reserve reaction functions and unpredictable timing of rate cuts versus prolonged pauses.
USD / FX

Currency markets are pressured by regional political risks and sovereign fiscal strain, notably in Europe, where debt concentration creates vulnerability for the euro.

KEY VIEWS

  • French sovereign debt spreads and deteriorating debt dynamics across government, household, and corporate sectors remain a localized epicenter of European financial risk.
  • Political intervention and unconventional currency dynamics could disrupt traditional safe-haven currency behaviors.

RISKS

  • Escalating fiscal strains in European core economies spilling over into broader currency instability.
  • Potential Treasury or monetary intervention altering global liquidity and mechanical dollar flows.
EQUITIES

Equity market leadership remains exceptionally narrow and concentrated in technology and semiconductors, while small-cap stocks face severe relative underperformance and multiple contractions.

KEY VIEWS

  • The current equity market narrowness mirrors the late 1990s, with a high number of S&P 500 components exhibiting negative betas that allow for alternative portfolio diversification.
  • Corporate earnings growth remains exceptionally strong, supporting large-cap resilience despite higher real interest rates.
  • Approximately 75% of companies with projected 5-year earnings growth rates of 25% or more are located outside the United States.

RISKS

  • Vulnerability to momentum unwinds if macroeconomic growth data or liquidity expectations shift abruptly.
  • Continued distress and underperformance in small-cap equities due to higher financing costs.
CREDIT

Credit markets show clear signs of dispersion and stress beneath the surface, with lower-tier credit categories experiencing substantial widening and multiple compression.

KEY VIEWS

  • Lower-rated credit instruments, such as Triple-C rated debt, have experienced significant sell-offs and wide spread widening.
  • Higher interest rates have already taken a measurable toll on risk assets through multiple contractions and credit dispersion.

RISKS

  • Further credit spread widening if corporate refinancing walls meet sustained higher borrowing costs.
  • Localized sovereign debt stress spilling over into broader regional credit markets.
AI / TECHNOLOGY

Artificial intelligence infrastructure buildout and private-to-public market value creation remain robust, supported by immediate capex expensing and collaborative safety regulatory frameworks.

KEY VIEWS

  • AI capital expenditure and infrastructure buildout are insulated from near-term midterm political shifts, with the bulk of physical deployment expected to be in place well before 2029.
  • Private markets enable early value capture across foundational AI layers—including intelligence, data organization, and financial infrastructure—with long-term compounding continuing post-IPO.
  • Industry leaders are actively working alongside governments to co-develop safety guardrails and regulatory frameworks in tandem with technological scaling.

RISKS

  • Execution risks and potential bottlenecks in balancing rapid technological scaling with stringent regulatory safety compliance.
  • Over-reliance on narrow hyperscaler cash flows supporting the broader technology trade.
COMMODITIES

Energy markets remain vulnerable to maritime security risks and shipping insurance costs in critical chokepoints like the Strait of Hormuz.

KEY VIEWS

  • Releasing refined fuel products from strategic reserves fails to address the underlying shipping insurance costs and maritime security threats driving energy price pressures.
  • Crude flows are already close to pre-conflict levels, rendering additional flow increases largely ineffective at moving market prices.

RISKS

  • Prolonged shipping disruptions keeping energy input costs elevated and feeding back into broader inflation metrics.
  • Geopolitical escalation impacting key Middle Eastern transit corridors.
