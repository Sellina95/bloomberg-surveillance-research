## Rates / Bonds

### Summary

Treasury yields and fixed income markets are facing structural headwinds from heavy corporate debt issuance linked to AI infrastructure buildouts, net investment-grade supply, and competing sovereign borrowing.

### Key Views

- Heavy debt issuance supporting AI compute and data centers has driven competition for capital and put upward pressure on Treasury yields (Peter Tchir).
- Vanilla investment-grade corporate debt is favored due to strong corporate balance sheets, whereas complex structured products carry hidden risks (Peter Tchir).
- Net investment-grade issuance is scaling up significantly, competing directly with Treasury and equity supply (Alex Altmann).
- Capital structure focus has favored senior debt instruments over preferred structures to reduce downside risk in volatile credit environments (Troy Gayeski).

### Risks

- Widening credit spreads on data center and tech-adjacent debt vehicles.
- Upward pressure on borrowing costs stemming from heavy sovereign and corporate debt supply.
- Potential liquidity crunches in complex structured credit products.

## Equities

### Summary

Equity markets exhibit strong positioning bias toward artificial intelligence themes, though underlying valuation concerns persist as capital expenditure outpaces immediate revenue generation.

### Key Views

- Big tech capital expenditures remain in a mid-cycle boom with substantial year-over-year growth, though bottlenecks are shifting toward electricity generation and power infrastructure (Lisa Shalett).
- Microsoft stands out among tech enablers due to accelerating cloud revenue growth, expanding user metrics for products like Copilot, and a commitment to maintaining free cash flow positivity (Stefan Slowinski).
- Ancillary AI plays create tracking challenges for institutional funds, making accurate exposure measurement difficult (Mark Lehmann).
- Shorting artificial intelligence remains widely viewed as a dangerous trade, keeping portfolio positioning heavily biased toward the long side (Alex Altmann).

### Risks

- Massive revenue deficits where collective AI ecosystem revenues fall far short of terminal breakeven capital expenditure needs (Alex Altmann).
- Uncertainty over whether massive AI capital spending will yield adequate return on invested capital for the spenders (Troy Gayeski).
- Non-trivial risk of a severe market correction in AI-driven wealth that could force rapid macroeconomic repricing (Gita Gopinath).


## Credit

### Summary

Credit markets are absorbing record amounts of net investment-grade issuance, with tightening spreads in vanilla corporate debt contrasting against wider spreads and heightened selectivity in tech-adjacent and data center debt.

### Key Views

- Tightening credit spreads have effectively assisted the Federal Reserve in tightening financial conditions and cooling inflationary pressures (Peter Tchir).
- Data center bonds are trading at wide spreads over benchmarks, reflecting growing scrutiny over financing structures and return profiles (Alex Altmann).

### Risks

- Refinancing and execution risk as debt-funded capital expenditures continue to expand.
- Potential spread widening across tech-adjacent debt if revenue generation lags behind debt service requirements.

## Cross-Guest Conflicts

### Federal Reserve Policy Stance and Rate Action Direction

**View A:** The Federal Reserve should actively raise interest rates using a risk-management lens to combat persistent upside inflation risks and second-round effects (Claudia Sahm, Patrick Harker).

**View B:** The Federal Reserve has the luxury of taking its time to bring inflation back to target amid economic uncertainty, without an urgent need for aggressive tightening (Gita Gopinath).

**Why It Matters:** Contrasting views from former central bank officials and economists highlight a deep division over whether current monetary policy is sufficiently restrictive, directly impacting rate expectations and fixed income volatility.

### Sustainability and Monetization of AI Capital Expenditure

**View A:** Selected enablers like Microsoft demonstrate clear cloud monetization, accelerating growth, and strong free cash flow self-sufficiency (Stefan Slowinski), supported by a mid-cycle capex boom backed by solid liquidity (Lisa Shalett).

**View B:** Immense capital expenditure requirements vastly exceed current ecosystem revenues, creating funding pressures, wide data center bond spreads, and unproven return on invested capital (Alex Altmann, Troy Gayeski).

**Why It Matters:** Disagreement over the health of AI monetization dictates whether massive tech spending is viewed as a productive multi-year investment cycle or an overextended bubble vulnerable to sudden re-pricing.
