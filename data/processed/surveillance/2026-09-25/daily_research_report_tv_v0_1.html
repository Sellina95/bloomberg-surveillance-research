EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
  • Unprecedented capital expenditure for artificial intelligence is driving massive global demand for capital, forcing real yields higher and altering traditional monetary policy transmission mechanisms.
  • Persistent input cost pressures—highlighted by diesel transportation expenses—are feeding directly into core inflation, complicating the path for central banks and maintaining a higher-for-longer rate environment.
  • Geopolitical friction, trade policy uncertainties, and supply chain reshoring initiatives continue to structuralize a shift away from lean efficiency toward economic resilience and security.

RATES / BONDS

Fixed income markets are grappling with sticky inflation, term premia build-up, and aggressive central bank pricing, as baseline interest rates normalize away from the ultra-low regime of the past decade.

KEY VIEWS

  • The current interest rate environment represents a historical normalization rather than an aberration, forcing investors to adapt to higher baseline yields.
  • Treasury buybacks are modest and targeted toward illiquid issues rather than functioning as aggressive yield suppression tools.
  • Asset managers are beginning to re-evaluate duration and length as bond yields offer genuine competition to richly valued equities.

RISKS

  • Unanchored term premia driven by midterm elections and heavy fiscal supply.
  • Persistent diesel and transport cost pass-through sustaining sticky core inflation.
  • Breakdown of traditional Treasury-equity correlations in a high-inflation regime.
USD / FX

Foreign exchange and cross-border capital flows are increasingly influenced by divergent national fiscal health, alternative emerging market outperformance, and structural trade friction.

KEY VIEWS

  • Paraguay exemplifies a strong regional outperformer with low inflation, high growth, and disciplined fiscal management while maintaining strategic alliances.
  • Greece demonstrates improving fiscal credibility and competitive tax regimes that successfully attract foreign direct investment and talent.

RISKS

  • Escalating trade policy disputes and potential retaliatory measures impacting global commerce.
  • Geopolitical instability threatening critical shipping channels and raising transportation costs.
EQUITIES

Equity markets remain heavily bifurcated, with mega-cap technology and AI-linked growth outperforming broader cyclical segments that struggle with higher borrowing costs.

KEY VIEWS

  • Regional investor sentiment and equity allocations remain exceptionally high, supported by positive corporate earnings revisions in mega-cap technology.
  • High equity valuations in top-tier technology firms serve as strong currency for funding private market rounds and infrastructure buildout.
  • Higher interest rates and inflation have driven multiple compression in broader equity P/E ratios.

RISKS

  • Vulnerability of richly valued equities to sustained upward pressure on real yields.
  • Macroeconomic divergence where rate-sensitive sectors underperform while tech-driven indices remain insulated.
CREDIT

Corporate credit spreads remain remarkably tight for non-financials, but mounting pressure is visible in lower-tier credits and opaque private credit markets.

KEY VIEWS

  • Non-financial corporate credit spreads have shown strong resilience despite higher benchmark yields.
  • Lower-quality corporate capital structures built during the zero-rate era are encountering significant debt-servicing friction.
  • Lower-end high-yield issuance has migrated to private credit, where risks are accumulating with less market visibility.

RISKS

  • Hidden default risks accumulating within private credit vehicles exposed to long-duration cash flows like software.
  • Widening credit spreads in riskier tranches if higher-for-longer interest rates persist.
AI / TECHNOLOGY

The artificial intelligence spending boom remains the primary engine of macroeconomic resilience, largely unhindered by traditional monetary policy transmission channels.

KEY VIEWS

  • Hyperscalers and AI-related economic activity are not interest-rate sensitive, insulating overall growth from central bank tightening.
  • State-backed capital investments in China continue to prioritize long-term technological leadership in advanced manufacturing and AI over short-term macroeconomic data.

RISKS

  • Supply chain bottlenecks and restrictions on critical minerals and semiconductor components.
  • Potential overextension of capital expenditure cycles if end-market monetization lags behind investment.
COMMODITIES

Energy markets are experiencing a structural transformation prioritizing security, spare capacity, and high inventory buffers over lean efficiency.

KEY VIEWS

  • Diesel export bans fail to create new fuel supplies, merely reallocating global scarcity and weakening domestic refining economics.
  • Geopolitical negotiations regarding energy infrastructure remain in a skeptical 'show me' phase among market participants.

RISKS

  • Disruptions to energy supply corridors driving up distillate spreads and core transportation inflation.
  • Political interference in fuel markets worsening global structural supply deficits.
